Why we measure the mental load, and refuse to score it

12 June 2026 · 8 min read · OmniKin product team

The fair-load view is the most-discussed screen in OmniKin and the one we rewrote most often. Five versions in, we had something honest that did not turn a household into a league table.

The thing we were trying to name

In most households, the work is not evenly split, and the unevenness is not really about who does more tasks. It is about who notices that a task exists. Someone keeps the running list of what has to happen this week, and that job is invisible until it is missed.

A chore chart cannot see this. It records completions. It says nothing about who decided the bins needed doing, who noticed the prescription was running out, or who remembered the school trip money.

Our first version counted completed tasks per person. It was accurate and completely useless. Everybody already knew the numbers, and seeing them made the conversation worse.

What we changed

Three decisions took the screen from provocative to useful.

  • Count the noticing, not just the doing. Creating and assigning a task is work. The view reflects that, so the person carrying the planning shows up in the picture.
  • Compare against the household, never against other households. There is no benchmark, no percentile, no "families like yours". The only reference point is your own previous weeks.
  • Never act on its own. OmniKin can suggest moving two evening tasks. It will not move them. A person confirms, or the suggestion quietly expires.

What we deliberately left out

No points. No streaks. No badges for children. Every reward mechanic we prototyped produced the same outcome in testing: the household optimised for the metric and stopped talking to each other. A calm tool has to be willing to be less sticky.

We also removed the weekly summary notification. It arrived on Sunday evenings and landed, in the words of one tester, like a performance review nobody asked for. The view is now something you go and look at when you want it.

Where it sits now

The fair-load view ships with the tasks module. It is opt-in per household, visible to adults by default, and it can be turned off entirely without losing anything else in tasks. If it helps you have one honest conversation and then you never open it again, we would call that a success.

★ Differentiator

No other family organiser we tested makes household load visible at all. The ones that come close do it as gamification, which is the opposite of what the problem needs.
